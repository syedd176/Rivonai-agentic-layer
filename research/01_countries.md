# Which European Countries Should This Product Launch In?

Market research for an English-only AI messaging agent for small businesses.
Written 26 September 2026.

**Every important claim below is labelled Confirmed, Likely, or Unclear.**
- **Confirmed** = an official body said it, or three or more independent sources agree.
- **Likely** = one reasonable source said it.
- **Unclear** = I could not establish it. I say what I looked for and where.

---

## 1. Headline findings

- **The English-only rule cuts Europe down to three or four real countries, not ten.** The United Kingdom and Ireland work. Malta works but is tiny. Everywhere else in Europe, the product would be asking a local plumber to answer their own neighbours in a foreign language. Most will not do that.

- **"Everyone here speaks English" is not the same as "our customers will accept being answered in English."** This is the single biggest trap in this market. The Netherlands is the best English-speaking country in the world by test score, and yet **67.6% of Dutch companies deal with the outside world in Dutch only** (Confirmed — Taalunie/Meertens Institute study). The businesses themselves have already made this choice, and the product cannot overrule it.

- **Even the most confident English speakers want their own language when something goes wrong.** 60% of people who read English most confidently still want customer service in their own language (Likely — CSA Research, 8,709 people in 29 countries, 2020). The product is used at exactly the moments people care most about: a price, a refund, a cancellation.

- **The prior report was wrong to disqualify Sweden and Denmark on WhatsApp numbers.** Those numbers are broadly right (WhatsApp ~49-50% in Sweden, ~30% in Denmark) but the conclusion does not follow. **Facebook Messenger is the number one messaging app in Denmark and number two in Sweden**, used by 65% of Swedes (Confirmed). There is a perfectly good channel mix in both countries without WhatsApp. **Sweden and Denmark fail on language, not on channels.** That correction matters because it means the team should stop scoring countries on WhatsApp.

- **The United Kingdom is the strongest market by a wide margin and it is not close.** Roughly 883,000 construction small businesses (Likely), WhatsApp reaching 90% of adults and Instagram 94% (Confirmed — Ofcom, May 2025), no language problem at all, tradespeople already paying £28-44 a month for job software (Likely), and a heat pump boom running at +112% year on year (Likely). Being outside the EU is an advantage here, not a drawback: no AI Act duty.

- **Ireland is the best EU option, and its retrofit boom is the single sharpest demand signal found anywhere.** Home energy upgrade applications are **up 96% in 2026, with individual grants up 186%**, and waiting lists of 12-24 months (Likely — Irish government / SEAI). Trades that cannot answer their enquiries are precisely the target customer.

- **Competitors already exist in the two best markets, priced at £49-99 a month.** This is not an empty field. Whoza.ai, TalkTrade and Grip sell to UK trades; AI Receptionist Ireland sells the same thing in Ireland at €69-297. In the Netherlands, **Blueprint already does almost exactly the described product in Dutch** — AI prices a quote and sends it by WhatsApp — at €249-699 a month. An English-only entrant would be both later and worse-fitting.

- **A cost change is coming on 1 October 2026 that the team must check before pricing.** Several sources say Meta will start charging for service replies inside the 24-hour window from that date; Meta's own documentation still describes them as free. I could not resolve this (Unclear) and it directly affects unit economics.

## 3. The English question — the most important section

The team asked the right question. Not "do businesspeople speak English," but **"will an ordinary member of the public, messaging a local plumber, accept being answered in English?"** Here is the honest answer per country.

### The general evidence first

**Confirmed** — English proficiency is genuinely very high in the candidate countries. EF English Proficiency Index 2025 (published November 2025, based on 2.2 million test takers in 2024): Netherlands 1st (624), Norway 5th (613), Denmark 7th (611), Sweden 8th (609), Finland 12th (603). All "very high proficiency."

**But proficiency is the wrong measure, and here is why.**

**Likely** — CSA Research, "Can't Read, Won't Buy" (8,709 consumers, 29 countries, 2020): 76% prefer to buy products with information in their own language; 40% will never buy from a website in another language; 75% are more likely to buy again if customer care is in their language. Critically: **even among those most confident reading English, 60% still prefer customer care in their own language.**

**Confirmed** — Eurobarometer: about 90% of EU internet users prefer to access websites in their own language, and 42% say they never buy online in a language other than their own.

The pattern is consistent. Ability to understand English is not the same as willingness to be served in it, and the gap widens exactly where money, complaints and refunds are involved — which is this product's whole job.

### Country by country

**United Kingdom — no issue whatsoever.** English is the native language of ~91.5% as a first language, 98.3% total (Confirmed — 2021 Census). A customer messaging a plumber in English is simply a customer messaging a plumber. **Verdict: Strong. No barrier.**

**Ireland — no practical issue.** English is the first language of ~85% and spoken by ~97.5% (Confirmed — CSO 2022 Census). Business is conducted in English essentially everywhere outside the Gaeltacht, and Gaeltacht residents are English-proficient too (Likely). Critically, **the Official Languages Act 2021 places Irish-language duties on public bodies only, not on private businesses** (Confirmed). A private plumber has no obligation to offer Irish. **Verdict: Strong. No barrier.**

**Malta — works, with a caveat.** English is co-official and spoken by ~88% (Confirmed — Census 2021 / Eurobarometer), and about 61% prefer English for reading and writing (Likely). English is the normal language of business and tourism. The caveat is that in everyday local retail, staff often greet in Maltese and the experience is genuinely mixed (Likely). An older or more traditional customer messaging a neighbourhood shop may expect Maltese. **Verdict: Moderate-to-Strong. Workable, but not the frictionless case Ireland is.**

**Netherlands — this is where the honest answer hurts.** Nearly everyone can hold an English conversation (~91%, Eurobarometer 2016; 93% per EF). And yet:

- **Confirmed** — Taalunie "Staat van het Nederlands" study (Meertens Institute and Ghent University): **67.6% of Dutch companies communicate with the outside world exclusively in Dutch.** Only 21.3% use English alongside Dutch. On customer contact specifically, 61.3% of employees speak only Dutch with customers and 65.5% email customers only in Dutch. This is the businesses' own revealed behaviour, and the product cannot override it — the owner is the buyer.
- **Likely** — Dutch market-entry guidance is blunt that comprehension is not trust: an English-language approach from an unknown brand "feels like a foreign party just passing through," raising doubts about returns, delivery and service. Native Dutch consistently outperforms English.
- **Likely** — Under Dutch consumer law, English terms and conditions "rarely pass the transparency test," because Article 6:238 of the Dutch Civil Code requires terms to be clear and understandable to a consumer, and courts are markedly less lenient with consumers than with businesses.

So: a Dutch consumer *can* read an English reply. But Dutch small businesses have already overwhelmingly chosen not to communicate that way, and the legal ground under consumer-facing English is soft. **Verdict: Weak on language. The Netherlands looks excellent on paper and is the country most likely to waste the team's time.**

**Sweden — probably not, and for a specific reason.** ~89% speak English (Confirmed — Statistics Sweden 2021). The Language Act (2009:600) makes Swedish the principal language but **binds public bodies, not private businesses** (Likely — I could not fully extract the statute text; the English translation PDF would not parse, so I am marking the private-sector exemption Likely rather than Confirmed). However:

- **Likely** — Swedish e-commerce guidance states product information must be in Swedish when selling into Sweden. This is a labelling and product-documentation rule, not a conversation rule, but it sets the expectation.
- **Likely** — Swedish sources cite 75% of consumers being more likely to buy where information is in their mother tongue and 74% more likely to buy again where online customer service is in their own language.

**Verdict: Weak-to-Moderate.** An English-only agent answering a Swedish consumer on behalf of a Swedish hantverkare would be unusual and would likely read as offshored.

**Denmark — same answer as Sweden.** ~86% speak English (Confirmed — Eurobarometer). Danish product labelling must generally be in Danish (or a language differing only slightly, i.e. Norwegian or sometimes Swedish) (Likely). No evidence found that Danish consumers expect or accept English from a local Danish trader. **Verdict: Weak.**

**Finland — actively discouraged by the regulator.** ~70% speak English, the lowest of the Nordics (Confirmed — Eurobarometer 2022 / Statistics Finland). And the Finnish Competition and Consumer Authority (KKV) states plainly: *if a company's online store is in Finnish, the consumer may assume customer service is also in Finnish; if customer service is provided only in English, this must be clearly mentioned in both marketing and the ordering process, as the practice may otherwise be misleading* (Confirmed). That is an official regulator describing this exact product pattern as potentially misleading unless disclosed. **Verdict: Weak, with an explicit regulatory warning.**

### Is there a legal requirement to serve consumers in the local language?

**Confirmed** — There is no blanket EU rule forcing customer service into the local language. EU law requires pre-contractual information to be "plain and intelligible," and the Consumer Rights Directive lets each member state impose its own language requirements for contract information — but states may also choose not to regulate it. The Court of Justice has held that national law cannot impose an *exclusive* language requirement where the directive says "easily understandable."

**So the risk is not a clean ban. It is three softer risks that add up:**
1. Product and safety documentation genuinely must be in the local language in Sweden and Denmark (Likely).
2. Consumer contract terms in English may be unenforceable in the Netherlands (Likely).
3. In Finland, English-only service must be disclosed or it may be judged misleading (Confirmed).

None of these stops an English agent in Ireland, the UK or Malta. All of them complicate the Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark and Finland.

## 5. The ranked top 5

### 1. United Kingdom

**Why it ranks here.** It wins on almost every axis simultaneously. The largest pool of target customers in Europe (~883,000 construction SMEs, 16% of all UK SMEs). Zero language friction. The best channel position of any candidate — WhatsApp at 90% of adults and Instagram at 94% (Confirmed, Ofcom May 2025), so the multi-channel design pays off immediately. Trades already pay for software monthly. A genuine, measurable enquiry boom. And being outside the EU means no AI Act Article 50 duty, which is a real cost saving on day one.

**Strongest argument against it.** The competition is already there and already cheap. Whoza.ai advertises an AI receptionist for UK trades that qualifies jobs and sends them to WhatsApp **from £59/month**; TalkTrade is £49-69; Grip runs £49-149. These are not adjacent products — they are the same product. The team would enter as a late follower against incumbents with local credibility, and the £49-59 entry price caps what can be charged.

**What would move it up or down.** Up: finding that existing players only answer phones and none actually holds a proper qualifying conversation across Instagram and Messenger, or none does the parts-in-stock / crew-free / inside-the-area check, or none has the owner-approves-before-sending step. That approval step is the most defensible piece of the described design and is missing from at least one competitor (Blueprint appears to send automatically). Down: discovering any of those incumbents has meaningful market share among the 883,000, or that trades will not pay above £59.

### 2. Ireland

**Why it ranks here.** The sharpest live demand signal found anywhere in this research. Home energy upgrade applications **up 96% in 2026, individual grants up 186%**, against waiting lists of 12-24 months and Warmer Homes waits of 24-26 months (Likely — Irish government / SEAI). That is the precise condition described as the ideal customer: a trade getting more enquiries than it can answer. Add no language friction, EU and eurozone membership, Stripe, and the highest share in the EU of e-commerce turnover through businesses' own websites (24.36%) rather than marketplaces — which is exactly the right shape for use case B.

**Strongest argument against it.** It is small, and it is already served. 81,043 construction firms is a fraction of the UK's 883,000, and 12,326 Shopify stores is a modest shop pool. AI Receptionist Ireland already sells WhatsApp, Instagram and web chat automation to Irish SMEs at €69-297/month and explicitly markets GDPR and EU AI Act compliance. Ireland alone cannot carry a company.

**What would move it up or down.** Up: treating UK and Ireland as one English-language go-to-market rather than two, which makes Ireland a cheap add-on rather than a separate bet, and confirming the retrofit boom is producing unanswered *messages* and not just unanswered phone calls. Down: finding that the retrofit surge is routed through grant portals and approved-contractor lists rather than direct customer enquiries, which would mean the enquiry volume never reaches the installer's inbox.

### 3. Malta

**Why it ranks here.** It is the only other country in Europe where English is genuinely co-official and where serving consumers in English is unremarkable. ~88% speak it; ~61% prefer English for reading and writing. It is in the EU and the eurozone, Stripe works, digital adoption is above the EU average (81.3% of Maltese firms have at least basic digital adoption, 17.3% using AI). Tourism is ~15% of GDP, which means a large base of small hospitality and retail businesses habitually serving English-speaking customers — an unusually good fit for English-only.

**Strongest argument against it.** Size, bluntly. Only 60,856 active enterprises in total and just 5,869 construction firms. Even perfect market share is a small business. It ranks third on *suitability*, not on *opportunity*.

**What would move it up or down.** Up: evidence that no competitor serves Malta at all, making it a cheap, uncontested proving ground for the English-only product before tackling the UK. Down: confirming that local shops default to Maltese with local customers, which would erode the one reason Malta is on this list.

### 4. Netherlands

**Why it ranks here — and it ranks on size, not on language.** The numbers are genuinely excellent: 273,508 construction firms of which 267,891 are micro (the largest micro-trade pool of any candidate except the UK), around 100,590-103,000 webshops, WhatsApp at ~92.9% and Messenger ranked second, Dutch trades already paying €20-200/month for quoting tools. On raw demand and channel fit it outranks Ireland and Malta easily.

**Strongest argument against it — and it is a strong one.** The language finding is genuinely bad, and it is the businesses' own choice, not a guess: **67.6% of Dutch firms deal with the outside world in Dutch only.** Two-thirds of the addressable market has already decided not to communicate the way this product communicates. Worse, the competitive position is the weakest of any candidate: **Blueprint already sells to Dutch small businesses exactly what the team described** — AI calculates a price from your rates, generates a PDF, sends it by WhatsApp — in Dutch, at €249-699/month. Watermelon (EU-hosted, ISO 27001, automatic language detection covering Dutch, English and French) and Trengo are established. And the Netherlands carries the heaviest rules burden: Article 50 plus a live ACM/AP chatbot guideline due to be finalised in autumn 2026.

**What would move it up or down.** Up: only one thing — adding Dutch. If the language constraint lifts, the Netherlands arguably becomes rank 2. Down: nothing needs to happen; it should not be launched into English-only as it stands.

### 5. Sweden

**Why it ranks here.** Mainly to correct the record. The prior report disqualified Sweden on WhatsApp reach, and that reasoning was wrong. WhatsApp is indeed only 49% (Confirmed — Svenskarna och internet 2025), but **Facebook Messenger is at 65%, Instagram at 65%, and Facebook at 71%**. Messenger ranks second among Swedish messaging apps. For a genuinely multi-channel product there is a viable mix without WhatsApp. On top of that, Sweden has the lowest marketplace reliance in the EU (24.78%), meaning its shops sell through their own sites — the right shape for use case B — and 99,266 micro construction firms with an established habit of paying 150-800 kr/month for quoting apps.

**Strongest argument against it.** Language, and only language. There is no evidence that a Swedish consumer messaging a local hantverkare will accept an English reply, and the mother-tongue preference figures cited in Swedish sources (75% / 74%) point the other way. Swedish product information is also legally required. A Swedish trade buying an English-only agent would be handing its own customers a foreign-language experience.

**What would move it up or down.** Up: Swedish-language support, or a narrow beachhead of Swedish businesses whose customers are already foreign — Stockholm and Gothenburg tourism, international schools, expat services. Down: any direct evidence that Swedish consumers react badly to English from a local firm would take it off the list entirely.

**A note on Denmark, which just missed.** Denmark's channel position is arguably better than Sweden's — Messenger is the number one messaging app there and Facebook reaches 84% of 16-74 year olds. It was cut below Sweden because of a specific practical problem: **section 10 of the Danish Marketing Practices Act bans unsolicited electronic marketing without prior consent, and it applies to B2B as well as consumers** (Confirmed). For a small SaaS company that needs cheap outbound to find its first customers, that closes the most affordable door.

## 6. Per-country detail

### United Kingdom

**Demand (A) — service businesses.** ~883,000-885,000 construction SMEs, 16% of all UK SMEs and the largest sector by business count at 15.8% (Likely — House of Commons Library / SME statistics 2025). Around 745,000 self-employed workers in construction as of Q3 2024 (Likely). The boom is real and measurable: a record 8,150 households applied for a heat pump in July 2026, **up 81% on June and 112% year on year** (Likely). Boiler Upgrade Scheme grants are £7,500 for heat pumps and £9,000 off-gas-grid, running to 31 March 2027 (Confirmed — Ofgem). The UK needs ~33,700 full-time heat pump installers short-term against current capacity (Likely) — a capacity squeeze, which is precisely what generates unanswered enquiries.

**Demand (B) — shops.** 702,776 live online stores (Likely — StoreLeads, 18 September 2026). 304,560 retail businesses as of 1 January 2025, over 99% of them SMEs (Likely). ~184,825 Shopify stores in England plus 10,308 in Scotland and 5,698 in Wales (Likely).

**Named competitors.** Whoza.ai (AI receptionist for UK trades, qualifies jobs, sends to WhatsApp, from £59/month). TalkTrade (£49/month annual, £69 monthly, asks job, postcode, urgency, access details, sends structured summary to inbox or WhatsApp). Grip (£49 for 75 calls, £99 for 175, £149 for 250 with calendar and bookings). Trades AI Solutions (answers in two rings, trade-specific qualifying questions, books into diary). Adjacent job-management incumbents: Tradify (£34-44/user/month), Powered Now (£28-40), ServiceM8, Simpro (£80+).

**Channel mix.** WhatsApp 90% of adults (up from 87% in 2024), daily use 76%; Instagram 94%; Facebook/Messenger 93% combined, though Messenger's own daily reach fell to 22% (Confirmed — Ofcom Online Nation, May 2025 data, published 10 December 2025). WhatsApp is the top messaging app and growing. Telegram: not significant, no figure found.

**Pricing expectations.** £28-99/month is the established band. £49-59 is the AI-receptionist entry point. Above ~£99/month the team is competing with enterprise field-service tools.

**Rules.** No AI Act. No UK AI statute as of 2026 — a principles-based, regulator-led approach under UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (Likely). There is no blanket AI-disclosure statute, but UK GDPR Article 5(1)(a) transparency and Articles 13-14 mean the customer must be able to tell they are talking to a machine from the interface, not from a buried privacy notice (Likely). The CMA has had direct consumer-protection enforcement powers since April 2025 with fines to 10% of global turnover (Likely). Marketing: corporate bodies can be emailed without prior consent; **sole traders count as individuals and require consent** (Likely) — a sharp catch, since sole traders are much of the target market. Cold calling is allowed unless the number is on the TPS/CTPS register.

### Ireland

**Demand (A).** 81,043 construction enterprises in 2023, of which **78,361 have 0-9 employees** (Confirmed — Eurostat sbs_sc_ovw / sbs_ovw_act, 2023). 96.7% of construction firms are micro enterprises; 206,446 people employed in construction, 8.8% of business-economy employment (Likely — CSO, Business in Ireland 2023). Overall, 401,359 active enterprises of which 371,480 are micro. The boom: applications for home energy upgrades **up 96% in 2026 with individual grants up 186% on Q1 last year** (Likely — Dept. of Climate, Energy and the Environment). National Residential Retrofit Plan 2026 has €558 million targeting 70,000 homes; heat pump grants to €12,500, solar PV €1,800, external wall insulation €4,500-12,000. Waiting lists 12-24 months, Warmer Homes 24-26 months (Likely).

**Demand (B).** 12,326 Shopify stores, up 8% year on year in Q1 2026 (Likely). Irish SMEs generate 30% of total sales via e-commerce against an EU benchmark of 20%; 51% of medium-sized firms sell online (Likely). Ireland records **the highest share in the EU of turnover through own website or apps at 24.36%** (Likely — Eurostat 2024), so Irish shops are not marketplace-dependent.

**Named competitors.** AI Receptionist Ireland (Irish SMEs, local numbers, phone + WhatsApp + Instagram DM + web chat + email/SMS follow-ups; AI Receptionist Calls €69-827/month, Chat Widget from €97, **WhatsApp Automation from €297** for 500 conversations; markets GDPR and EU AI Act compliance; trades listed as a target vertical). VoiceFleet (AI receptionist for local service and field-service businesses, Ireland trades content). Irish lead platforms that shape expectations: Tradesmen.ie, Tradesman.ie, LocalTradesmen.ie.

**Channel mix.** WhatsApp penetration >90% of internet users as of Q3 2024 (Likely — Statista, 90.1%). Facebook 2.60m users (63.4% of adults), Instagram 2.50m (60.9% of adults), **Facebook Messenger 1.65m (40.2% of adults)** (Likely — DataReportal Digital 2025: Ireland, 3 March 2025). 98.9% internet penetration. 75% of people used social platforms in the prior three months, up from 73% (Confirmed — CSO Household Digital Consumer Behaviour 2025). All four target channels are live; this is a genuinely multi-channel market.

**Pricing expectations.** €69-297/month is already being charged to Irish SMEs for this category. Irish SMEs are heavy SaaS users — one figure puts SME spend at US$11,200 per employee annually (Likely, and I would treat it cautiously as it is a global SME figure, not Irish-specific).

**Rules.** EU AI Act Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. Marketing: B2B email to business addresses relating to work activity is permitted; **cold calling requires consent unless a previous business relationship exists** (Likely). No Irish-language obligation on private firms (Confirmed).

### Malta

**Demand (A).** 5,869 construction enterprises, of which **5,630 have 0-9 employees** (Confirmed — Eurostat 2023). Total 60,856 active enterprises in 2024, up 3.1%; 73.2% are sole proprietors or partnerships; micro firms (0-9) grew 2.9% (Likely — NSO Malta, Business Demography 2024, released 26 November 2025). 146,420 registered business units in 2025 on the statistical register, but that is a register count and much larger than the active-enterprise figure — do not use the two interchangeably.

**Demand (B).** No reliable online-shop count found for Malta (Unclear). Tourism is ~15% of GDP with a large small-business hospitality and retail base habitually serving English speakers (Likely).

**Named competitors.** None found targeting Malta specifically (Unclear — this may mean genuinely unserved, or may mean my searches were not in Maltese; I searched in English only for Malta, since English is co-official).

**Pricing expectations.** Unknown (Unclear). Eurozone, Stripe supported. Malta Enterprise runs a "Digitalise Your SME" grant scheme covering cloud and AI adoption (Likely) — a possible subsidised route in.

### Netherlands

**Demand (A).** 273,508 construction enterprises, of which **267,891 have 0-9 employees** (Confirmed — Eurostat 2023). This is the second-largest micro-trade pool among candidates. Heat pump demand is real: Techniek Nederland expects the installation-sector staff shortage to roughly double to 40,000 people, and about 50% of heating engineers need retraining (Likely). Installers reported more quote requests from March 2026 onward (Likely). Note the nuance: the Dutch bottleneck is described mainly as a *labour and skills* shortage, with the year-long heat pump waits expected to clear — a weaker enquiry-overflow signal than Ireland's or the UK's.

**Demand (B).** Around 100,590 webshops in Q2 2025 per CBS projections — the first quarter CBS expected a decline — with the net number since stabilising just above 103,000 (Likely). Largest categories: fashion (23,770) and general assortment (21,040). Over 2,200 webshops are Thuiswinkel.org members representing over 70% of online consumer spending, so the long tail is large but commercially thin.

**Named competitors — the densest field of any candidate.** **Blueprint** (blueprint-solutions.nl) is the direct threat: "De Totaaloplossing voor het Nederlandse MKB," an AI Offerte Generator that calculates prices from your rates, generates a professional PDF and sends it by WhatsApp or email, plus CRM, invoicing, calendar and website; Starter €249/month to 200 conversations, Professional €499 to 1,000, Enterprise €699 unlimited, Voice Agent +€199. Notably its quotes appear to send automatically with no owner-approval step — the one clear gap. **Watermelon** (Dutch, AI-first, chat/email/WhatsApp/social with unified inbox, EU-hosted, ISO 27001, automatic language detection in Dutch/English/French; pricing on request). **Trengo** (Utrecht, shared inbox plus automation, WhatsApp/email/social). **Aanloop AI** (AI receptionist plus WhatsApp agent, live in 14 days, from €597/month). Trade-specific: JustRunBiz Buitendienst (jobs, planning, work orders, quotes, invoices, iDEAL and WhatsApp), Gripp, Veldwerk, Cobry. General Dutch AI chatbot pricing sits at €49-299/month with €500-2,500 one-off setup (Likely).

**Channel mix.** WhatsApp 92.9% in 2025 (Likely — Statista), ~87% of population. Facebook Messenger ranks second among messaging apps in the Netherlands (Likely). Excellent multi-channel fit.

**Pricing expectations.** €150-699/month observed for AI agents; €20-200/month for trade quoting software. Dutch vendors lean demo-led with setup fees, which suggests **self-serve is less established here than in the UK** (Likely) — a problem for a SaaS-not-consulting ambition.

**Rules — the heaviest of any candidate.** EU AI Act Article 50 from 2 August 2026. On top of that, the ACM (consumer and markets authority) and AP (data protection authority) issued a joint opinion in October 2025 warning that **access to human contact is under pressure, that people regularly cannot tell whether they are talking to an employee or a chatbot, and that chatbots give incorrect or misleading answers**; they called chatbot reliance "one of the biggest annoyances" for consumers and said chatbots should not fully replace humans. Consultation input closed 17 May 2026, a draft guideline was published summer 2026, and **the final guideline is expected in autumn 2026 — i.e. now** (Confirmed on the process and timing; I could not fully extract the opinion PDF text, so the precise obligations are Likely rather than Confirmed). Marketing: B2B outreach is workable via legitimate interest with an opt-out (Likely). Consumer contract terms in English risk failing the Article 6:238 transparency test (Likely).

### Sweden

**Demand (A).** 105,772 construction enterprises, of which **99,266 have 0-9 employees** (Confirmed — Eurostat 2023). No specific retrofit or heat pump enquiry boom found for Sweden (Unclear).

**Demand (B).** **Only 24.78% marketplace usage — the second-lowest in the EU after Estonia's 16.51%** (Likely — Eurostat 2024 via Cross-Border Magazine). Swedish shops sell through their own sites, which is the right shape for use case B. Malta, Denmark and Sweden lead the EU in manufacturers selling via own website (Malta 22.2%, Denmark 20.9%, Sweden 20.7%). No total Swedish webshop count found (Unclear).

**Named competitors.** No exact match for the described product found in Swedish (Moderate competition, not weak). Adjacent: Freshchat via Scaly.se (live chat, WhatsApp, Messenger, omnichannel; Growth Omnichannel from $29/agent/month, Pro $39-59, Enterprise $69-99, Freddy AI Copilot +$29). Chatfuel ($59/month, 1,000 conversations, WhatsApp). Trade quoting tools that set price expectations: Smidia (179 kr/month PRO, ROT deduction built in), Offeaze (from 289 kr/month), Minuba (Minuba GO free up to 2 users), Hantverksappen, Hantverksdata, Easoft. General Swedish AI chatbot pricing 990-4,990 kr/month, with simple small-business options from 200-1,000 kr/month (Likely).

**Channel mix — the correction to the prior report.** Svenskarna och internet 2025 (used in past 12 months): **Facebook 71%, Facebook Messenger 65%, Instagram 65%, WhatsApp 49%, Snapchat 37%, Telegram 4%** (Confirmed). WhatsApp has grown fastest, from 36% in 2022 to 49% in 2025. 85% of Swedes use social media daily. Messenger is the messaging app most Swedes use and is stable over time. **Telegram at 4% is negligible — drop it for Sweden.** Also relevant: 65% of Swedish consumers prefer digital self-service to phone customer service, and 35% sometimes or always avoid phone and in-person contact with companies (Likely — Customer Experience Benchmark, Toluna for Smart Communications). That is a tailwind for messaging-based service, if the language problem could be solved.

**Pricing expectations.** 150-800 kr/month per user for trade quoting software; 990-4,990 kr/month for AI chatbots. Roughly €15-80/month at the small end — **lower than UK or Irish tolerance.**

**Rules.** EU AI Act Article 50 applies. Language Act 2009:600 makes Swedish the principal language but binds public bodies (Likely). Swedish required for product information when selling into Sweden (Likely). Marketing: consent needed for email absent a prior business context; cold calling permitted unless the contact is on the NIX register (Likely).

## 7. What I could not find out, and how to close each gap

1. **Whether consumers in the Netherlands, Sweden or Denmark would actually accept an English reply from a local business.** No survey asks this question directly. I found strong indirect evidence — Dutch firms' own 67.6% Dutch-only behaviour, CSA's 60% figure, the Finnish regulator's warning — but nothing that asks a Dutch or Swedish consumer "would you mind if your plumber answered in English?" **How to close it:** run it yourself. A 300-person panel survey in each of NL, SE and DK costs a few thousand euros and would settle the single most important question in this report. Alternatively, buy the country-level cuts of CSA Research's "Can't Read, Won't Buy" — the 29 countries surveyed include the Netherlands, Sweden and Denmark, and the cuts exist but sit behind their paywall (contact crwb@csa-research.com).

2. **Whether WhatsApp service messages stay free after 1 October 2026.** Sources conflict directly. Several vendor analyses say Meta will begin charging per business message including service replies inside the 24-hour window from 1 October 2026; Meta's own pricing documentation still describes non-template messages in the customer service window as free and says the October change is rate adjustments, not a model change. This materially changes unit economics. **How to close it:** read Meta's official rate cards and pricing changelog directly at developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing, and ask a Business Solution Provider (360dialog, Twilio, CM.com) in writing.

3. **Whether self-serve signup or sales meetings are the norm for small-business software in each country.** I found nothing systematic. The only usable signals were indirect: UK trade SaaS advertises free trials and per-month pricing (self-serve), while Dutch AI vendors quote setup fees of €500-2,500 and "live in 14 days" (sales-led). **How to close it:** check the sign-up flow on 10 local competitor sites per country. If the primary button is "Start free trial," self-serve is normal; if it is "Book a demo," it is not. That is an afternoon of work and it directly determines whether this can be a SaaS business in that country.

4. **Malta's channel mix and shop count.** No Malta-specific platform penetration or online-store figures found. **How to close it:** DataReportal's Digital 2026: Malta report, and NSO Malta's structural business statistics for retail.

5. **The exact obligations in the Dutch ACM/AP chatbot guideline.** The PDF would not parse cleanly and the final guideline was due autumn 2026 — it may already be published. This is the most immediately actionable regulatory item for the Netherlands. **How to close it:** check acm.nl and autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl now for the final guideline.

6. **Whether the Swedish Language Act definitively exempts private business.** The official English translation PDF would not extract. I am confident it binds public bodies, but I could not quote the statute. **How to close it:** read the Swedish original at riksdagen.se, or ask Språkrådet (the Language Council).

7. **Whether Ireland's and the UK's retrofit surges produce unanswered *messages* specifically.** I confirmed application volumes and waiting lists, but application counts are not the same as enquiry volume hitting an installer's inbox. **How to close it:** interview 10 installers in each country. Ask how many enquiries they get weekly, how many they fail to answer within a day, and on which channel. This is the actual demand test and no published statistic substitutes for it.

8. **Denmark's platform-by-platform figures.** Danmarks Statistik publishes Facebook 84%, Instagram 56%, Snapchat 45%, LinkedIn 33%, TikTok 19% (16-74, 2023) but **does not break out Messenger or WhatsApp separately**, which are the two that matter most here. The 80% Messenger figure I found is from a commercial source, not the statistics office. **How to close it:** Statistikbanken table KV2SM1, or DST's media-use enquiry service.
